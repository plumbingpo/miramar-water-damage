Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Miramar, Miramar Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

The cleanup process involves removing all standing sewage, extracting contaminated water from structural materials, removing and properly disposing of every porous material that contacted sewage (carpet, padding, drywall, insulation), thoroughly disinfecting hard surfaces, applying antimicrobial treatments and completing structural drying to prevent secondary mold growth. To finish, thermal foggers and hydroxyl generators of industrial grade deodorize the space so the sewage smell does not linger.

Our Sewage Cleanup Process

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

1

Hazard Check and Containment

Our team assesses the extent of sewage contamination, identifies safety hazards and establishes containment to prevent cross-contamination of unaffected areas.

2

Extracting Sewage and Removing Materials

Standing sewage is pumped out completely. Contaminated porous materials including carpet, padding, drywall and insulation are removed and properly disposed of as biohazard waste.

3

Cleaning and Disinfecting

We thoroughly clean every surface that remains, then disinfect it with an EPA-registered antimicrobial solution. Meanwhile, air scrubbers fitted with HEPA filters pull pathogens and other contaminants from the air.

4

Dry-Out and Deodorizing

Residual moisture is taken out with commercial-grade dryers. Sewage odors are eliminated through industrial deodorizing. Finally, a clearance test verifies that people can safely use the area again.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together
  • People in the household getting stomach or intestinal symptoms with no clear cause
  • Dark residue from sewage left on the basement floor or ringing a drain
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Foul-smelling moisture showing up on flooring or along the walls
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

No homeowner should try to clean up sewage personally. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Roots from trees close to the sewer line work their way into pipes and block them, so have them cleared
  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Address slow drains promptly, before they become complete blockages
  • Schedule a camera inspection of the sewer line every 2 to 3 years so trouble is found early

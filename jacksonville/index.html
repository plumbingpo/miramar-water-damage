Preventing Water Damage in Jacksonville Homes

Houses tell you when water is getting where it should not. Our technicians see the same early clues again and again in Jacksonville, and they are worth learning. Blistering paint, ceiling stains, warped baseboards and floorboards that curl at the edges each point to water behind or beneath them. Add a stale odor in an enclosed space, a loose toilet, or a faucet that keeps dripping.

Taken singly they are minor, though every one of them gets more expensive with time. Renew the caulk around baths and showers once it splits, because water that slips behind tile stays hidden for a long time. Test a sump pump several times a year by filling the pit until the float trips and the motor runs. In basements and garages, shelving and plastic tubs keep belongings clear of the floor.

No amount of maintenance prevents every leak. Fewer and smaller leaks are still a real gain, especially compared with a Jacksonville home full of drying machines for most of a week.

Typical Water Damage Problems in Jacksonville

Water trouble is not all equally urgent, and knowing the difference helps you decide what to do first. We have ordered these five common Jacksonville calls by how fast each one needs attention.

Bursts: A pipe that has split is the most urgent call there is, because it puts out water for as long as the supply is on. Cold weather, depending on the season, is one trigger, with the leak appearing as the frozen section thaws. Shut the house supply, run a tap to drain the lines and phone us immediately, whatever the hour. If a ceiling is sagging from the weight of water, stay out from under it and do not poke it.

Runoff: Storm water coming under a door or through a garage is next in line. There is no valve to close here, so lift what you can off the floor and avoid wet electrical outlets. This water has crossed lawns, driveways and streets, which makes it unsanitary. The Miramar Restoration team extracts it once conditions are safe, then removes soaked materials that cannot be cleaned.

Kitchens: Leaks from a dishwasher, an ice maker line or the plumbing under the kitchen sink should be dealt with the day you find them. Turning off the supply valve for that appliance normally ends the leak itself. What remains is the water trapped below the cabinets and floor covering, and it will not dry by itself. In Jacksonville kitchens we often dry cabinets in place by sending air through small holes in the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Dampness in the space below the floor can wait a few days, though not a few months. Puddles on the ground cover, dripping ductwork or wet insulation between the joists are what an inspection usually turns up. Left alone, the damp air rises into the rooms above and the wood framing begins to decay. Our crew clears any standing water, brings the framing down to a normal moisture level and reports the likely cause.

Odors: A musty smell near the site of an old leak is the least urgent item here, yet it deserves an inspection soon. Mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a material getting wet, so a leak from months ago had plenty of time. Do not scrub or spray a large patch yourself, because disturbing it spreads spores through the house. Our technicians test the area, contain it and remove the growth along with the moisture feeding it across Jacksonville.

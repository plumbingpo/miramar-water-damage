Basement Waterproofing Guide in Miramar, FL

Home / Blog / Basement Water Prevention Tips in Miramar, FL

Your home's structural integrity, indoor air quality, and resale value all depend on keeping the basement dry. Even so, plenty of Miramar, FL houses have wet basements, with problems that run from a little seepage up to a full flood. This guide from Miramar Restoration covers the usual causes of basement water intrusion and the fixes that keep Miramar basements dry and healthy.

What Makes a Basement Wet

There is more than one route for water to reach a basement, so the first job is to find out which one you have. Soaked soil presses on the foundation, and that hydrostatic pressure drives moisture through any crack, joint, or pore in the concrete. Clay soils swell when wet, and that sideways pressure can open cracks in a foundation wall where water then enters. Surface water from poor grading, clogged gutters, or weak drainage runs against your foundation and finds a way in. A basement can also be damp without any leak at all, because humid warm air condenses on cool walls and floors.

Waterproofing From the Outside

The most effective long-term waterproofing deals with water from the outside. Exterior foundation waterproofing means excavating around the foundation, applying waterproof membrane coatings, and installing or upgrading the exterior drain tile. No other method costs as much, though none protects as thoroughly, since the water is stopped where it starts. Regrading is also worth the money, because ground that falls 1 inch or more per foot across the first 6 feet away from the foundation carries water off.

Interior Waterproofing Solutions

Inside systems do not stop water at the wall; they control it once it has come through. A French drain installed inside runs around the basement edge, gathers the water, and routes it into the sump pit to be pumped out. During heavy rainfall and outages, a sump system does the work, and a battery backup is the ideal setup. Interior waterproof coatings and sealants give some protection against minor seepage and efflorescence, but they do not replace proper drainage when water intrusion is significant.

Sump Pump Care and Testing

For Miramar homeowners with sump pumps, proper maintenance is essential. Every three months, fill the pit with water until the float trips and confirm the pump runs. Pay attention to any strange noise, since it may mean a part is failing. Because outages are when basement flooding is most common, a battery backup is worth installing. For more protection still, think about a second, water-driven pump. Make sure the discharge line sends water at least 6 feet from your foundation and has a check valve to prevent backflow.
